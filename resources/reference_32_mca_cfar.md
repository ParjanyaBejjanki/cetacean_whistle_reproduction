## Abstract

This paper proposes a detector design method for extracting the frequencies of whistles produced by cetaceans, which are underwater marine mammals. There are two considerations when extracting whistles. First, each whistle must be detectable when multiple whistles occur simultaneously. Second, only whistles should be detected when whistles and clicks are mixed. Considering these points, we designed a window along the frequency axis in the time-frequency domain and improved the MCA-CFAR (minimum cell averaging-constant false alarm rate) detector. Applying the proposed detection method to real data shows that it is effective for whistle detection.

## I. Introduction

Cetaceans, underwater marine mammals, produce various acoustic signals underwater. The sounds generated by dolphins and whales are largely of two types: echolocation clicks and whistles. Clicks are broadband impulse signals and are used for prey search, etc. Whistles are nonlinear frequency-modulated signals and are known to be used for individual identification and communication among cetaceans [1]. In addition, some species produce continuous and sometimes coded impulse signals such as codas and burst-pulsed calls [1].

Recently, biomimetic communication technologies that utilize these whale sounds have been developing [2]. Biomimetic communication mainly mimics whistles and clicks, and applies modulation schemes such as CSS (chirp spread spectrum), OFDM (orthogonal frequency division multiplexing), and FSK (frequency shift keying) to cetacean acoustic signals [2]. Since covertness is important in biomimetic communication, the degree of mimicry of biological signals becomes an important metric.

To maximize the degree of mimicry in biomimetic communication, a process is needed to extract the features of cetacean acoustic signals from a large amount of data. In particular, for whistles, frequency information over time is important. The STFT (short-time Fourier transform) of a received signal is one method that can confirm the frequency of a whistle over time. Since a whistle forms a local maximum on the frequency axis of the STFT, it can be detected through CFAR (constant false alarm rate).

When applying a detector to the STFT to extract the frequency of a whistle, two points must be considered. The first is the situation where whistles and clicks are mixed. In this case, the detector should be designed to detect only whistles while avoiding clicks as much as possible. Since clicks are impulse signals, it is more effective to detect them separately from whistles in the time domain. The second is the situation where multiple whistles exist. This problem appears when multiple dolphins produce whistles simultaneously.

This paper proposes a whistle extraction method based on MCA-CFAR (minimum cell-averaging CFAR). The proposed method is a modification of the existing MCA-CFAR and is suitable for application when multiple dolphins simultaneously produce whistles. In addition, the proposed method has the characteristic of avoiding click detection.

## II. Detector Design

In this paper, we propose a 1D MCA-CFAR detector along the frequency axis for whistle extraction. The proposed method is designed considering the two points mentioned in the introduction. First, to avoid detecting clicks, reference cells were designed along the frequency axis. Since click energy is spread across the frequency band, the detector regards clicks as noise. Second, to detect multiple whistles, an MCA-CFAR scheme was applied that removes cells with large signal magnitudes when estimating noise from the reference cells. MCA-CFAR estimates noise after removing large values from the reference cells through the step-size setting [3]. However, this detector has a problem in that the threshold increases significantly when two local maxima are formed at a frequency spacing corresponding to the step-size.

Therefore, in this paper, two MCA-CFAR thresholds are used simultaneously. This scheme has the property that even if one threshold increases, the other threshold can compensate by detecting the whistle.

Specifically, the proposed method derives MCA-CFAR for two different step-sizes and then performs a minimum operation for each cell to obtain the final threshold. If the two step-sizes are \(d_1\) and \(d_2\), respectively, and the thresholds of the MCA-CFAR detectors for each step-size are \(T_1(n)\) and \(T_2(n)\), then the threshold \(T(n)\) of the detector proposed in this paper can be obtained by the following equation.

$$
T(n) = \min\{T_1(n), T_2(n)\}. \qquad (1)
$$

## III. Whistle Detection Results

The performance of the proposed method was verified using data provided by NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration) [4]. Figure 1 shows detection results in a situation where multiple whistles overlap, and Figure 2 shows multiple detection results in a situation where whistles and clicks are mixed.

In Figures 1 and 2, (a) is the STFT, and (b), (c), and (d) are the detection results of CA-CFAR, MCA-CFAR, and the proposed MCA-CFAR, respectively. The false alarm probability of CA-CFAR was set to \(10^{-6}\), and the guard cell size and reference cell size were set to 6 and 22, respectively. (c) is the MCA-CFAR detection result, with the reference cell size and step-size set to 24 and 14, respectively. (d) is the detection result of the proposed method, with the reference cell size set to (24, 82) and the step-size set to (14, 66).

As can be seen in Figures 1 and 2, the proposed method detects even the parts that appear as missed detections in CA-CFAR and MCA-CFAR. Since all detectors set reference cells along the frequency axis, it can be seen that clicks are not detected, as can be confirmed between 0.9 and 1.4 seconds in Figure 2(a).

---

## IV. Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an MCA-CFAR detector capable of detecting the frequencies of whistles in situations where multiple whistles and clicks are mixed. As a result of applying it to real data, it was confirmed that the detection results remain valid even when whistles overlap, and it was shown that clicks are not detected.
